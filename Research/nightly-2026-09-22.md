## The exemplar that changed its claim about the world was moved by an axis it does not sit on

Exemplar #107 is `T3 K2 A4 C4 R2 D3 S3 P1 E3 L3 G…`, and its G position went from G4 "Throughput
stays inside" to G3 "Molecules clear late" — two different worlds, drawn from the same 17 events.
Nothing wrote either of them. G3 and G4 carry **exactly equal priors in the registry on all 30
published nights since the G axis arrived at r8**, they moved by the same −0.00007 tonight, and
**G took zero evidence tonight**: its whole 0.1314 pp is the grounding widener.

The sampler draws a position by walking a cumulative sum against one uniform (`axes.py:3484`),
and `exemplars()` reuses a fixed seed, so each exemplar's uniform is the same number every night
and only the cell boundaries move. Under this line's conditionals — D3, E3, P1, R2 and S3 all
fire on G — the boundary between the G3 and G4 cells sat at 0.535482 on the live registry and
sits at 0.535906 tonight, because **G2 rose 0.0380 pp and pushed everything after it**. A uniform
in `[0.535482, 0.535906)`, a band 0.000423 wide, was G4 and is now G3. That is 4.2 draws in
10,000, and #107's is one of them.

So a reader watching this exemplar would see one claim about benefit realisation replaced by
another, on a night when no evidence touched either claim and the position that moved is a third
one. **37 of the 120 exemplars sit on the G3/G4 pair**, and the pair has never separated. The
reading is the same family as every other defect this project has found: a surface whose change
does not mean what it looks like. Held, never applied — the prior is the parent's.
