# Review of the sheet, 2026-09-01 — the readout describes one line as fact

**Read against** build `20260901-1130`, registry `r8-2026-08-20`, rendered from `docs/` at
1720 px with Playwright at 2028, 2035, 2050 and 2077.5 on every tab; 2,960 passages composed
offline (40 exemplar lines × 74 years) with `describe()` and `headline()`; every authored table
counted against the registry. Recommendations are held for August's review; nothing here is
applied.

## Summary

The prose at the head of the sheet is vague for a structural reason, and no amount of sentence
polishing reaches it. The headline and the bullets describe **one sampled world-line, whose
joint probability is 0.00075%, in declarative past tense, assembled by hash from 2,897 authored
vignettes.** They never state a probability. The actual forecast — the marginals, the crossing
years with their spread, the 68% on a superhuman researcher by 2035, what today's evidence
moved — lives on dials, chart annotations and control buttons, in small type, while the largest
lettering on the sheet reads "Bankruptcy judges in Delaware handed server halls to bondholders."

The recommendation is to **rebuild the readout as a forecast statement derived from the model's
own quantities**, with a fixed skeleton organised by variable, probability in every sentence
that can carry one, and the authored layer cut to one calibrated sentence per position per
horizon (183 sentences, from the registry's own descriptions, against 2,897 strings today).
Section 3 gives the design and a worked mock at 2035; section 5 gives the plan in six phases,
each shippable alone. Section 4 is the moderate alternative, which fixes the same defects inside
the present design and which I recommend against on its own.

Six bugs surfaced on the way and are correct to fix under either option (finding 6).

## 1 · Findings

### F1 · The headline states no probability and describes one line as fact

The 2028 headline, with nothing pinned:

> In 2028, AI is reorganising software teams and research groups first, because their work
> already arrived as text a machine could check. Households in Virginia and Georgia pay higher
> electricity bills as utilities connect data centres faster than they build generation.
> Bankruptcy courts in Delaware take filings from the operators of half-built computing halls
> whose lenders refused a further draw. Three United States frontier laboratories buy the
> emptied halls at a fifth of build cost, since the lenders holding them want any buyer. Safety
> staff resign when their veto becomes a recommendation, and product managers inherit the
> release decision.

Every sentence is an authored consequence of E3 (valuation correction, 32%), S1 (23.7%), L4
(22.6%) and T2 (33%), stated as what happens. `mainline.json` gives the joint probability of the
line being described as `7.5e-06`. Nothing on the passage says it is one line, nothing gives a
number, and the one line that would let a reader decode it — `A2·C1·D2·E3·G4·K1·L4·P3·R4·S1·T2
at 2077` — is the last bullet on the sheet and is written in registry letters.

Across the sweep, 26% of headline sentences carry a number of any kind, and none of those
numbers is a probability. Meanwhile the chart's crisis labels ("68% SUPERHUMAN AI RESEARCHER BY
2035", "32% THE BUBBLE DEFLATES HARD") are the best-written forecast sentences on the sheet, and
they are 1.7 mm annotations.

### F2 · The specifics are authored, and no gate can check them because nothing derived them

The project's rule is right — *every clause says a checkable thing* — and the implementation
turned it into *every clause carries a proper noun*. Delaware, Oslo, West Africa, Ontario and
Denmark, "a machinist's daughter [who] commutes ninety minutes", "Radiographers in Oslo hold a
personal clearance before opening the software their hospital bought. When one lapses, the
scanner idles." These read as checkable and are not: no quantity in the model produced them.
`prose_gate.py` (26 KB, eleven rule families) polices *style* — invented institutions,
metaphors, closing formulas, Strunk 14 — and cannot police *provenance*, because the strings
have none. The authored corpus is 2,897 strings and 78,858 words: HEADCL 1,080 clauses, FRAG
360 stage texts, RUNG_SHORT 126, CROSS 128, ECON_MOD 70, ECON 60, TENSION ~120, PAIRS 15,
MARKERS 13, LONGFORM 54 entries. Nine `apply_*.py` passes and a replay script exist to rewrite
it. That is the shape of a system fighting its own architecture.

### F3 · Consecutive years hop subjects, and within a line the sentences still repeat

Headline slots rotate on `(year × 7 + hash) % list.length`, so a reader stepping the index reads
unrelated subjects year on year. The mainline, 2038 to 2041:

> **2038** … Radiographers in Oslo hold a personal clearance … Health ministries across West
> Africa screen patients with software no local body can inspect.
> **2039** … Editors at the largest medical journals reject papers whose authors cannot share
> data across the line. Retraction notices now cite export law.
> **2040** … Actuaries write the losses into pension valuations, and public boards in Ontario
> and Denmark raise contribution rates … Grid engineers on the western interconnection now plan
> around four customers.
> **2041** … Apprenticeship places in the electrical and nursing trades fill faster than
> university ones … When one supplier raises its tariff, the cost of radiology reporting and
> legal drafting moves in every country the same week.

The variation machinery (three alternatives per stage, `altOf` salt, `vary()` shape rotation,
rotating `procAt`) was built to defeat repetition and produces incoherence instead. And it does
not defeat repetition: holding one line and stepping all 74 years, the passage says 5,785
sentences of which 770 are distinct — **each sentence read 7.5 times** — and the "Transition
speed" group is identical from 2031 to 2036. The most common sentence on the sheet, in 100% of
passages, is "At 3.0 a machine writes better code than any human engineer; at 4.0 it runs its
own research." 30% of passage sentences carry neither a number nor a proper noun. A passage
averages 78 sentences and 1,119 words.

### F4 · The model runs out at 2050 and the prose does not say so

The registry defines its positions through 2050: T4 is "2037 to 2050", T5 "uncrossed through
2050-12-31", A7 "transfers past 2040". The ladder tops out at 6.0 and the median reaches it in
2043 (p90 in 2035); 80 of the 120 exemplar lines are flat from 2050 to 2100. Every track is a
compounding rate with a hard cap — 60,000 GW, $30 trillion, −35% employment, approval falling
0.3 points a year to a floor of 8 — and by 2077 the spread has collapsed onto the caps: revenue
p10 = p50 = p90 = $30.0 trillion, employment p10 = p50 = −35%. So the sheet letters, at 2077:

> Installed AI compute is 52,361 GW. That is more than twice all the electricity generating
> capacity that existed worldwide in 2026. … AI revenue is $30.0 trillion a year. … Employment
> is 35% below 2026, larger than any peacetime fall on record. Employment has been flat for five
> years. … 43,895.8M human-equivalent years of work per year, 12 times the world's human labour
> force.

52,361 GW is about six times world generating capacity; the band's lowest rung is 20,000 so the
caption reads "more than twice". A reader who knows the domain stops trusting the document at
any one of these. The stage clock's fifth and sixth stages exist to fill the 55 years the model
cannot reach, which is why the far texts were always the vaguest: there is nothing behind them.

### F5 · The skeleton hides the model's factorisation

The passage is organised in four lanes from 2026-08 — System capabilities / Build-out and
governance / Capital and employment / Oversight and public opinion — with group heads like
"Transition speed", "Gains realised", "Index and rate", "Crossings ahead". The model is eleven
variables, and the reader operates eleven controls. T, the master variable, is the *last*
paragraph, unlabelled ("Capability trajectory"); K, R, L and G get one group each; the
capability paragraph mixes T, K and G. "Index", "ladder", "line", "rung", "span", "stage" are
internal vocabulary. The gate bans "this line" in a literal and cannot see `crossingClause`
generating "This line reached general superhuman capability in 2033, 44 years ago."

### F6 · Silent gaps and wrong readouts

1. **Three of the six CROSS slots never fire.** `describe()` calls `X('C','S')`, `X('E','S')`
   and `X('T','C')`, which look up `C1|S1`, `E3|S1`, `T2|C1`; the table holds `S1|C1`, `S1|E1`,
   `C1|T1`. Zero of 40, 0 of 25 and 0 of 40 pairs resolve. The three that do are thin: E×D 7 of
   20, A×T 18 of 35, P×D 6 of 20. Nothing reports a missing clause.
2. **K4 has no HEADCL or FRAG text**, so a line with K4 (5%) drops its takeoff clause silently.
   `registry-covered.json` declares K4 covered. The coverage gate tests the declaration, never
   the tables.
3. **The alternatives plate hard-codes nine axes** — `['T','K','A','C','R','D','S','P','E']` —
   so every panel label omits L and G, two of the eleven variables a reader just set.
4. **"What the setting moves hardest by 2040" prints AI EMISSIONS on 42 of 61 buttons** (T1
   "−642 MT", every K, six of seven A, seven of eight C) because emissions have the widest
   scale and always win the ratio. All six G buttons print the identical "AI REVENUE −1.2 $T",
   since G enters no track. The figure under a button is the one thing a control teaches, and
   it is a proxy.
5. **The Research tab still draws the 26 evidence-programme figures against r4 letters** under
   r8 names (documented in HANDOFF on 08-18; thirteen rows attach a figure to a position that
   now means something else).
6. **The 30-day drift is a registry artefact.** The lookback baseline lands on 2026-08-02, before
   r5 rebuilt the position space, so the largest "drifts" are E2 −25pp, A3 −21pp, P3 −20pp — the
   rebuild, drawn as movement.

### F7 · What already works, and should set the register

The record year. The 2026 headline:

> The best model METR has timed finishes 17.4 hours of expert work at even odds, though agents
> complete only 16% of real freelance jobs. The four largest hyperscalers guide to roughly
> $725–745bn of capital spending this year, against about $410bn in 2025. Brussels postponed
> its high-risk duties to December 2027 … Roughly 420 local moratoria now stand in force …

Measured, dated, attributed, and it commits. The registry's position descriptions have the
same quality ("Frontier systems run the AI research loop end to end by 2028-12-31. Arithmetic
on METR's published rates carries a 50% time horizon from 16 hours on 2026-05-08 to one working
month of 167 hours on 2027-03-05 at the 89-day doubling"), and so does This Morning's plate.
The forecast years should read like the record year, with probabilities and ranges where the
record has measurements. The material is all emitted already: marginals with history, a
2,000-line ensemble, crossing-year spreads, six crisis probabilities, four dated claims, 144
edges with twenty written `cond_stories`, and every morning's applications with their drivers.

## 2 · Where I disagree with the standing rules

- **"The passage and the headline are composed, never selected."** Right instinct, wrong
  material. Compose from the numbers. A sentence composed from authored fragments is still a
  selection, made by a hash.
- **"A year gets its variation from its own arithmetic and from a dated calendar."** Half right.
  The calendar ends in 2030 and the arithmetic saturates by the mid-2040s. Past that the honest
  variation is none, and the sheet should say less, and say why.
- **The stage clock.** Running each line's prose at its own pace is elegant, and it is the
  mechanism that puts stage-6 fiction at 2077 on a T2 line. A forecast has horizons, and a
  horizon is a date.
- **The four-lane skeleton.** August's brief was "across our different variables and controls".
  Organise by variable.
- **"Every clause says a checkable thing."** Keep the rule; change the test. Checkable means
  derived from a quantity, a date on the record, or a citation — and the gate should refuse a
  clause that is none of the three unless it is marked as illustration.

## 3 · The design: a forecast readout

### Principles

1. Every sentence at the head of the sheet is derived from a quantity, a date on the record, or
   a citation. The one exception is a marked illustration, at most one per variable.
2. Probability is stated wherever the model has it. Blue is probability in motion; the headline
   is blue; the headline carries probabilities.
3. The skeleton is fixed — same shape every day, every year, every pin — so a change is visible
   as a change. Variation comes from the numbers, which is where it truthfully is.
4. Forecast register throughout: "in 67% of sampled futures", "on the likeliest path", "the
   median crossing". No bare past tense for a future year.
5. The horizon is the registry's horizon. Past 2050 the readout says the registry defines
   nothing and stops composing.
6. The reader is entitled to see which kind of claim is which — the project's own principle,
   extended to *derived* against *authored*. A small mark per bullet: model, record, meaning.

### The headline — four sentences, one shape

1. **Capability at the year, with probability and spread.** From the ensemble at Y: the share of
   sampled futures past each rung, the median crossing, the quartiles.
2. **The likeliest settings, with their weights** — or the pinned ones, named as pinned.
3. **The nearest test on the register**, or when a control is pinned, the control that moves the
   crossing most.
4. **What moved today**: count, net movement, the largest single application and its driver.

Mock at 2035, mainline, from today's data (figures computed from `exemplars.json`,
`marginals.json`, `delta.json`, `claims.json`):

> By 2035, AI runs its own research end to end in 67% of sampled futures; the median crossing is
> 2034, and a quarter of futures cross before 2031 and a quarter after 2044. The likeliest path
> crosses in 2029–31 (33%) with the two rungs inside one year (51%), a valuation correction
> (32%) and delivery gated by reliability (36%). The nearest registered test is whether the
> state AI-law count exceeds 90 by 31 December 2026, which supports a contested patchwork (25%).
> Six applications this morning moved the network 0.83 points; the largest raised near-misses
> recur by 0.21 after Anthropic reported training an Opus-class model on 80 real
> reinforcement-learning environments.

Four sentences, four probabilities, a range, a date, a citation, and every noun introduced. It
changes when the year changes, when a control changes, and when the morning changes — because
the numbers do.

### The blocks — one per variable, registry order, five kinds of line

`T K A C R D S P E L G`, three columns balanced by the existing partition. Each block:

| line | source | example (T at 2035) |
|---|---|---|
| **head** | registry name + its question, top two positions with %, 30-day drift | CAPABILITY TEMPO · when do frontier systems first run the AI research loop end to end? · 2029–31 33% · 2032–36 28% |
| **distribution** | marginals at Y, or SET | 2029–31 33% · 2032–36 28% · 2037–50 17% · method asymptote 12% · 2027–28 10% |
| **quantity** | the track this axis drives, at Y, p10/p50/p90 across the ensemble, with a five-year rate | 67% of sampled futures have crossed the research rung by 2035; median 2034; superhuman coding 84%, median 2031 |
| **meaning** | one authored sentence for the modal or pinned position at this horizon, from the registry description and the dossier | On the likeliest path the research loop closes in 2029–31: METR's 50% horizon reaches a working month at the 89- to 129-day doubling by 2027 to 2028, and this position prices a doubling near 300 days |
| **evidence** | the latest application touching this axis, dated, with its driver | 25 Aug: a benchmark result on the unbranded Ox Alpha model moved 2029–31 by +0.15 points |
| **edge** | the strongest conditional into or out of this axis given the pinned or modal parent, in the `cond_stories` wording | Under a 2027–28 crossing, near-misses and undetected failures both become likelier because there is less time to check anything |

And D at 2035, to show a quantity-led block:

> **DIFFUSION AND LABOUR** · how much paid work do machines take, and how fast? · reliability
> gates delivery 36% · broad absorption 25%
> ▪ Reliability gates delivery 36% · Broad absorption 25% · Delivery stalls 24% · Displacement
> shock 15%.
> ▪ Employment at 2035: 5% below 2026 on the median future, 22% to 1% across the ensemble. AI
> revenue $4.9 trillion a year, $1.0 to $17.8 trillion.
> ▪ On the likeliest path reliability gates delivery: firms route machine work to where a
> mistake is cheap to catch and undo, entry-level hiring in exposed occupations runs 13% below
> comparable unexposed roles, and incumbent employment is flat.
> ▪ 25 Aug: Thomson Reuters launched its own model for legal work; reliability gates delivery
> +0.42 points.
> ▪ Under a displacement shock, backlash governs becomes 1.6 times likelier.

Eleven blocks of four to six lines is about fifty lines, against 78 sentences today, and every
one of them is derived except the *meaning* line.

### The authored layer, shrunk to what only writing can do

One MEANING table: 61 positions × 3 horizons (near ≤ 2031, mid 2032–2040, long 2041–2050) =
183 sentences, each written from the position's registry description and its dossier, each
carrying the position's own figure, each in forecast register. This replaces HEADCL, FRAG,
RUNG_SHORT, CROSS, ECON, ECON_MOD, TENSION and PAIRS. The strongest vignettes move into the
position's LONGFORM entry, which opens on click, marked as illustration. The `prose_gate` stays
and runs over the MEANING table, where its rules now fit the material.

### Horizon

The slider and the chart end at **2050** (or 2060 with the last decade lettered EXTRAPOLATION).
The chart's knee moves from 2040 to 2035 so the decade the model actually discriminates gets
the width. Past the horizon the readout composes one line: "The registry defines no position
past 2050. Quantities beyond it are the track equations run out, with the spread collapsed
onto their caps." I recommend 2050. Keeping 2100 keeps 55 years of decoration on a document
whose whole argument is that it draws only what the engine emitted.

### Register and vocabulary

"Sampled futures" for world-lines; "milestone" for rung; "path" for line; "the likeliest path"
for the mainline; no "span", "stage", "index" in any lettering a reader sees. Verbs in forecast
mood. A pinned control is named as a supposition: "With the tempo fixed at 2027–28, …".

## 4 · The moderate alternative, and why not on its own

Keep the composed passage and: fix the six bugs; put the crossing probability and the
composition, decoded, in the headline's first and last sentences; replace every "measured
quantity" with the ensemble's p10/p50/p90; fix the headline slot order so the same slot always
carries the same kind of claim; delete stages 5 and 6 and the far span; add a provenance mark
per bullet. About two days.

It buys a better version of the same problem. The vignettes remain unfalsifiable, the
variation still comes from a hash, the gates still test style, and the sheet still describes
one line of 121 million as what happens. Every fix in this option is also phase 0 or phase 3
of the plan below, so nothing is lost by choosing the full design.

## 5 · Beyond the passage

- **Controls.** Give each axis its natural readout under a button: T the median crossing shift
  and P(research rung by 2035); K the months between rungs; A the ladder shift in years; C the
  US compute share; R measures in force; D employment; S installed GW; P approval; E revenue.
  L and G enter no track: print the edge they tilt hardest ("tilts near-misses recur ×1.3")
  and say so, which is true and teaches something. Retire the seven-quantity argmax.
- **Alternatives.** Derive the label from the registry; caption each panel with how it differs
  from the likeliest path in words ("later crossing, verified limit holds, displacement
  shock") and its ensemble weight.
- **Research.** Withdraw the 26 r4 figures from the sheet or re-key the 23 that have a
  destination; HANDOFF already found three that belong withdrawn.
- **Drift.** Baseline the 30-day lookback on the latest registry version date, so a rebuild
  reads as a rebuild and the dials show the world.
- **Method.** Carry the numbers the readout now derives from: ensemble size, horizon, the
  caps, the exemplar count, and what a "sampled future" is.
- **Cases for the parent, held.** Track caps and the linear approval decline, which produce the
  2077 figures; a `horizon` field in the registry so this sheet reads it rather than assumes it;
  K4's prior at 5% with no authored meaning anywhere; L and G entering the tracks.

## Appendix · How the numbers were produced

- Rendering: `docs/` served locally, Chromium 1194 via Playwright, viewport 1720 × 1400,
  `__FW.sheetState()` read after two animation frames.
- Passage sweep: `describe()` and `headline()` imported from `narrative.js` in Node 22, over the
  first 40 exemplar lines and years 2027–2100; a sentence counts as carrying a number if it
  contains a digit, a proper noun if a capitalised word follows a lowercase one.
- Saturation: `bands.json` annual percentiles; `exemplars.json` tracks at 2035, 2050, 2077.
- Table coverage: registry keys from `network.json` against `HEADCL`, `FRAG`, `PROCESS`,
  `LONGFORM`, and the CROSS and ECON_MOD key sets.
- Control readouts: `__FW.effectsFor({})` in the built page, intervention mode.
