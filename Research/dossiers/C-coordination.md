| fact | figure | date |

| White House national AI legislative framework recommending **federal preemption** of state law | issued | **20 March 2026** |

**The observed regime is fragmentation, and the attempt to consolidate it has so far failed.**
145 state laws against one federal law is not a patchwork forming — it is a patchwork that has
formed, with a live and unresolved attempt to displace it.

## 2 · Mechanism

The model's C axis is written mostly about *international* coordination (US–China deals,
moratoria). The strongest 2026 evidence is *domestic*, and it bears on C directly: a state that
cannot consolidate its own internal rules is a poor candidate for negotiating and verifying an
international one. Domestic fragmentation is a **leading indicator** for C3's difficulty and a
direct instance of C4.

The weakest step in the C3 (verified deal) chain remains **verification**, and the S dossier
sharpens why: the thing you would verify — advanced packaging — sits in a single jurisdiction
that is itself the object of the dispute.

## 3 · Resolution criteria

| position | resolves TRUE if, in the year in question |
|---|---|
| **C1** none / race | no binding international instrument on frontier training, **and** no single national programme controls >50% of frontier compute |
| **C2** US securitization | US federal law or executive action preempts state AI regulation **and** imposes clearance or licensing on frontier developers |
| **C3** verified deal | a US–China (or wider) instrument in force with **declared training compute and third-party site access** |
| **C4** fragmented blocs | ≥3 jurisdictions enforcing materially different frontier rules, with no preemption and no international instrument |
| **C5** moratorium | training above a stated capability threshold prohibited in jurisdictions covering >70% of frontier compute |

## Recommendation

Current priors: **C1 0.402 · C2 0.264 · C3 0.074 · C4 0.251 · C5 0.009**

The 2026 record is a direct read on C4 and it says C4 is **currently the actual state of the
world domestically**: 145 state laws, one federal law, preemption attempted and not passed.
C1 at 0.402 is doing a lot of work as "nothing coherent is happening", but a great deal is
happening — it is simply fragmented rather than absent. Those are different worlds and the
model has a position for each.

Recommended: **C1 0.36 · C2 0.27 · C3 0.08 · C4 0.30 · C5 0.01** — held for review.

C3 stays low and the S dossier explains why better than the scenario literature did: the
verification target sits inside the disputed jurisdiction.

## Gaps

- **G10 · C conflates "no coordination" with "no coherent coordination".** C1 reads as absence.
  The record shows abundant, incoherent, sub-national regulation — which constrains labs, moves
  work between jurisdictions, and is nothing like absence. C4 partly covers it and C1's prior
  is carrying the rest.
- International evidence in this dossier is **thin**. A second round should cover the
  US–China channel, the EU AI Act's implementation record, and any compute-declaration
  proposals. Marked as a **partial** answer to question 1.
