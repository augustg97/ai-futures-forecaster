nobody chose it.

**The rule that follows: a nightly build has no business committing a tree it did not make.**
`nightly.sh` should commit `docs/` and the pulled data, and refuse the run — or stage nothing
else — when `git status` shows unstaged work under `web/js` or `build/`. A scheduled task that
runs at a fixed hour will meet a live session eventually, and tonight it did.

I stopped after the second run and left the tree alone. The live session owns it and its own
build will overwrite `docs/` with a state someone chose.

## K4 has never had a clause

The registry's takeoff axis carries four positions. **`FRAG` carries three.**

```
FRAG      K1+ K2+ K3+ K4-        HEADCL  K1+ K2+ K3+ K4-        LONGFORM  K1+ K2+ K3+ K4-
```

K4 — "gap past five years", prior 0.04977 — has no row in any of the three authored tables, and
has had none since the axis was created. The table has been exactly one row short of the registry
at every commit since `1ed5580` on 17 August: 48 rows against 49 positions at r5 and r6, 54
against 55 at r7. Four nights, two rebuilds, four published sheets, every gate green.

**What a reader gets.** `stageText(undefined)` returns an empty string, `PROCESS` covers only
C1–C8 so the transition-speed row has no second source, and `grouped()` drops a row whose text is
empty. So the row leaves the sheet. Composed at 2035 on the mainline with only K moved:

| K | rows in "System capabilities" |
|---|---|
| K3 | Current capability · **Transition speed** · Index and rate · Crossings ahead |
| K4 | Current capability · Index and rate · Crossings ahead |

The reader sets a control that is drawn on the sheet, and the sentence about what they set
disappears. Not blank — absent, with the paragraph closing over the gap.

**How often.** 225 of the 2,000 ensemble lines are K4, **11.25%**, against the 4.98% prior; the
conditionals push it up. 15 of the 120 exemplars are K4, so the Alternatives tab, which draws
twelve, is very likely to show at least one line that says nothing about takeoff. The mainline is
K1, which is why the default view has never shown it.

**The mechanism, and it is this project's own signature.** `registry-covered.json` declares K4.
The coverage gate compares the pulled registry against that declaration and passes. But the
declaration is itself an authored claim, hand-written, and **nothing checks it against the
tables it claims to describe.** The 08-19 finding was that a coverage declaration protects only
what it declares. Tonight's is one turn further in: a declaration can assert coverage that does
not exist, and the gate built to catch drift will confirm it.

The check that finds this is four lines — for every position in the pulled registry, require a
row in `FRAG`, `HEADCL` and `LONGFORM` — and it is the same check the r7 session ran by hand on
the axis it had just written, when it measured L drawing in 100% of passages. A new axis gets
verified. The verification is not kept.

I have not written the K4 clauses. Three stage variants of FRAG, a HEADCL row and a LONGFORM row
are authoring, and a gate that refuses the publish until they exist would stop tomorrow's nightly
on an unattended run. Both belong to a session with someone at the keyboard.
