## The explicit-date floor is already crossed; rounding is all that is holding it

The Atlas ratchet refuses when `events.explicit_pct` falls more than a point below the frozen
baseline (`Research/modeling/audit_all.py:270`), and the baseline recorded 2026-07-31 is 91.7,
so the floor is 90.7 and the comparison is a strict `<`. Tonight's run reports 90.7 and passes.

Counted from the staged trunk rather than read off the summary line:

```
n=2456  explicit=2227  fell_back=229  pct=90.6759  → rounds to 90.7
```

The corpus is at 90.6759%. It is already 0.024 points **below** the floor; the only thing
standing between this project and a refused publish is `round(100 * expl / n, 1)` in
`check_events`. Adding a single event without an explicit date takes the rounded figure to 90.6
and the gate fails — measured, not estimated: the runway is one event.

The composition explains the slide and confirms it is not a data defect. The trunk grew 2,432 →
2,456 last night, and 19 of those 24 arrivals carried an explicit date — about 79%, against a
standing corpus at 90.7%. Incoming events are less well dated than the events already held, so
the percentage falls roughly a tenth of a point a night. Held at 79%, or at 85%, the gate refuses
on the next ordinary night. It would need incoming events at 91% to hold the line.

**Nothing here is this project's to fix.** The corpus, the baseline and the ratchet are the
Atlas's, and the Atlas's own nightly task owns the failure when it comes. The designed behaviour
is already correct: on a refusal this build stops and the sheet keeps serving `20260904-1130`.
What is recorded here is that the refusal is now due, and due on the next night rather than
eventually.

## The drift printer went silent on the night the slack ran out

`audit_all.py` has a day-over-day printer, added 2026-08-03, precisely for this — it was written
because "a metric could erode night after night and pass silently as long as it stayed inside the
tolerance", and its comment cites `explicit_pct 91.8 → 91.7` as the erosion that went unremarked.
Tonight it printed nothing, on the night the metric spent the last of its tolerance.

It compares against `baselines.json:last_run`, and `last_run` is overwritten by whichever
invocation of the gate ran first that night. The Atlas's own nightly ran ahead of this one and
wrote `last_run.date = 2026-09-04, explicit_pct = 90.7`. The gate this project ran then read the
same corpus, found `now == was`, and skipped the entry. The step that mattered — 90.8 → 90.7,
the one that closed the margin — was never printed in the run this project saw.

This is the window-label family again, in its sixth sighting: **a baseline that outlives the
population it was set against.** `last_run` is named for the last run and used as though it meant
yesterday, and the two stop agreeing the moment a day holds two runs. A second reader of an
unchanged corpus is structurally blind to that corpus's drift. The fix belongs in the Atlas —
key the comparison to a date earlier than today, or record per-date rather than per-run — and is
noted here for whoever holds that decision.
